# Drop sourcing — recreating the 4 reference sets on Tapstitch

Reference: a 4-look "bestsellers restock" grid (Strawberry Matcha, Leopard Print Tracksuit,
Burgundy Pink, Navy Capri). Each look is a **two-piece set**: a fitted cotton tank plus a
matching bottom with contrast trim.

**Research caveat:** tapstitch.com is blocked by this session's network egress policy, so the
catalogue could not be browsed page by page. Every SKU below comes from indexed Tapstitch
product pages and their own copy. SKU numbers, names and described construction are real;
**prices, current availability and colourway options are unverified** — confirm each on the
site before committing.

---

## The four looks, decomposed

| Look | Top | Bottom | Signature detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strawberry Matcha | cotton halter tank, green w/ pink contrast binding | fold-over waist straight leg, green w/ pink side stripe | fold-over waistband + side stripe |
| Leopard Print Tracksuit | cotton racer tank, brown w/ leopard binding | tracksuit pant, brown w/ leopard fold-over waist + white piping | leopard trim as cut-and-sew, not print |
| Burgundy Pink | cotton halter tank, burgundy w/ stripe binding | semi-flare straight leg, burgundy w/ striped fold-over waist | striped rib waistband |
| Navy Capri | racerback tank, white/navy colour block | fold-over wide leg capri, navy w/ white side stripe | cropped capri length |

Three of four share the same two mechanics: **a fold-over waistband** and **contrast trim in a
second fabric**. Solve those two and the whole drop is one pattern block in four colourways.

## The two real gaps

### 1. No fold-over waistband exists in their catalogue

Searched directly and found nothing. This is the signature detail on **three of the four looks**
— the folded contrast waistband is what makes them read as a set rather than as separates.

Options, in order of preference:

1. **Custom development.** Tapstitch runs a "Fashion Collection Development" service alongside
   POD. A fold-over waistband on an existing block (RK0015 or WB0029) is a straightforward
   pattern modification, not a new garment. This is the ask to put to them.
2. **Substitute the detail.** Ship RK0015 as-is with its elastic drawstring waist and let the
   contrast side stripe carry the set language. Loses the Y2K low-rise fold entirely — which is
   most of the reference's appeal.
3. **Drop to two looks.** Burgundy Pink (semi-flare, WB0029) and Leopard (tracksuit, RK0015)
   depend least on the fold-over. Launch those two, develop the fold-over for a second drop.

### 2. No women's capri length

Only `UB0009` Unisex Plaid Loose Capri Shorts turned up, which is a short, not a capri pant.
The Navy Capri Set needs either a custom crop on WB0088 / RK0072, or the look ships full-length
and stops being a capri.

### 3. Leopard trim is cut-and-sew, not print

Tapstitch prints DTG and DTF, so an all-over leopard is easy. But in the reference the leopard
sits on the **waistband fold and the neckline binding** — separate trim fabric, not a print on
the body. Printing it would read as a different garment. This is another custom-dev ask.

---

## Recommended approach

**Build the drop on two blocks, not eight SKUs.**

- **Block A — halter tank**: WB0001 (contrast binding halter) in 4 colourways.
- **Block B — straight/flare pant**: RK0015 (contrast side stripe) in 4 colourways, plus WB0029
  where a flare is wanted.

That gives all four looks from two patterns, with colour and trim doing the differentiation —
which is exactly how the reference brand is operating. Add WQ0004 as the tracksuit jacket and
WB0014 as the short, and one development cycle yields a 6-piece drop.

Then put **one** custom-development request to Tapstitch: a fold-over contrast waistband on the
RK0015 block. That single modification unlocks three of the four reference looks and is the
difference between a generic athleisure set and the Y2K silhouette that is actually selling.
