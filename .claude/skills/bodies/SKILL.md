# bodies

A consumer fitness/wellness brand around a **compact, portable, premium-looking smart
Pilates board** — a Pilates studio that lives in a small apartment.

Carried over from prior ChatGPT work. **Do not restart the branding from zero.** The
name, product direction, three colorways, target customer and visual benchmark are all
already decided.

## Who you're working with

Alex. Non-developer, moves fast, hates being talked at. See the `kerberos` skill for the
full working relationship — it applies here identically. The short version:

- **Produce the thing, don't describe the thing.** "Make it", "generate", "show me" means
  build it now. Explaining what you would make is a wasted turn.
- **Strong opinions, not menus of options.**
- Casual tone is fine. Long theory is not.
- **"Accurate" has a specific meaning:** match the established product geometry closely,
  not a generic approximation. It is the single most repeated correction in this project.

## The product in one line

A long, low-profile rounded-plastic Pilates board with two padded supports, two rail/cable
tracks, resistance cables with handles and ankle straps, two black adjustable anchor posts,
a central circular control, and a **hinged screen at the rear that folds down for storage**.
Accessories match the colorway.

It must read as *"a Pilates board with smart technology"* — **never** as a shrunken
commercial reformer, a Peloton clone, or a sci-fi gym device.

Hard constraints: a woman must be able to carry it. Premium consumer-electronics plastic,
not cheap toy plastic, not metallic luxury. Mass-market, not €1,500+ studio equipment.
Never invent mechanically impossible features to make a render look futuristic.

## The brand

Lowercase **bodies** wordmark, rounded geometric sans. Gen-Z wellness, Pilates-girl
culture, beauty-brand confidence. Feminine without cliché, premium but attainable.

Not a gym. Not bodybuilding. Not an old-school studio. Not clinical. Not girlboss. Not a
beige minimalist wellness brand with no personality.

The customer is not buying resistance training. She is buying **a Pilates studio at home** —
sculpting, toning, posture, a routine, and a beautiful object she wants in her apartment.
Lead with Pilates / sculpt / tone / move; the resistance mechanics sit underneath.

## The look

**Clean structure + aggressive colour + editorial photography + lots of breathing room.**

Visual benchmark: the **Experiment Beauty** homepage — borrow its colour play, white-space
rhythm, bold black type, saturated blocks and Gen-Z energy. Never copy its skincare content.

Rhythm: white → saturated colour → white → photographic colour → white → neon. Not every
section gets a coloured background.

Photography is the highest-priority element: **aesthetic Instagram Pilates girls** in
beautiful apartments, bedrooms, sunlit living rooms. Contemporary workout sets, glowy skin,
natural poses. Never generic gym stock, never middle-aged corporate wellness, never beach
or tropical — **the brand is fundamentally at home**.

Handwritten annotations used sparingly, as editorial markup, never childish doodles.

**Gen-Z is not "make everything neon."** It comes from styling, photography, unexpected
combinations, confident copy and intentional colour.

## Copy voice

Short. Confident. Playful. Internet-native. Never corporate. Sell the feeling first:
*"I want this in my apartment."*

Established territory (direction, not locked taglines): PILATES AT HOME · A STRONGER,
HAPPIER YOU · MORE THAN A WORKOUT · ONE BOARD. SO MANY WAYS TO MOVE · SMALL SPACE. BIG
CHANGE · WORKOUTS THAT MOVE WITH YOU · SAME STUDIO FEELING. DIFFERENT LOCATION.

Avoid: girlboss, "that girl", forced TikTok slang, "obsessed", emoji spam.

## Corrections already made — do not repeat them

Earlier website attempts were rejected for being **too generic, too summer/beach, not
playing with colour enough, lacking Gen-Z photography energy, and not breathing** like the
Experiment reference.

Feedback decoder:
- *"more vibrant"* → raise intentional colour contrast and saturation
- *"too summer"* → drop beach/outdoor; use colourful home interiors
- *"not Gen Z"* → better styling, composition, colour blocking, social-native imagery
- *"doesn't breathe"* → more white space, more section spacing, less clutter
- *"too generic"* → more distinctive editorial art direction
- *"accurate"* → return to the established product geometry
