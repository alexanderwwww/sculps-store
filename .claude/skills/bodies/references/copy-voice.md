# BODIES COPY & VOICE

## Voice
Short.
Confident.
Playful.
Modern.
Feminine.
Internet-native.
A little cheeky when appropriate.
Never corporate.

## Copy philosophy
Sell the feeling first.

The customer should think:
“I want this in my apartment.”

Then:
“It can replace/augment my Pilates studio.”

Then:
“It also gives me sculpt, strength, stretch and recovery.”

## Customer language
Prefer:
- Pilates.
- Sculpt.
- Tone.
- Stronger.
- Feel good.
- Move.
- Routine.
- Studio at home.
- Small space.
- No commute.
- Full body.
- Guided workouts.

Use technical terms only when they help explain the product.

Avoid leading with:
- Resistance mechanics.
- Hardware specifications.
- Exercise science jargon.
- Hardcore performance language.

## Headline territory
- PILATES AT HOME.
- A STRONGER, HAPPIER YOU.
- MORE THAN A WORKOUT.
- ONE BOARD. SO MANY WAYS TO MOVE.
- NO GYM. NO LIMITS.
- WORKOUTS THAT MOVE WITH YOU.
- SMALL SPACE. BIG CHANGE.
- MOVE BETTER. LIVE BRIGHTER.
- YOUR STUDIO. YOUR SPACE.
- SAME STUDIO FEELING. DIFFERENT LOCATION.

- JOIN BODIES

## Product description direction
Example:
“Your Pilates studio, without the studio. bodies brings guided Pilates, sculpt, strength and recovery into one compact machine designed to live beautifully at home.”

This is a direction, not final locked copy.

## Social proof
Testimonials should sound like real customers:
- Short.
- Specific.
- Casual.
- Focused on convenience, aesthetics, feeling stronger, and small-space usability.

Avoid suspiciously perfect corporate testimonials.

## Gen-Z copy rule
Be culturally aware, not cringe.

Avoid:
- “Girlboss.”
- Excessive “that girl.”
- Forced TikTok slang.
- Overuse of “obsessed.”
- Fake youth language.
- Excessive emojis.

The design and photography should create most of the Gen-Z feeling; the copy should stay confident and clean.
