# BODIES BRAND

## Name
**bodies**

Always use lowercase “bodies” in the visual brand unless a context specifically requires uppercase.

## Why the name works
The name is:
- Simple.
- Memorable.
- Feminine without being overly girly.
- Broad enough for a movement/wellness ecosystem.
- Compatible with Pilates, sculpt, stretch, strength, and community.
- More culturally current than predictable fitness names.

## Brand architecture
Potential ecosystem:
- bodies Pilates
- bodies Sculpt
- bodies Stretch
- bodies Club

The master brand remains **bodies**.

## Brand personality
Think:
- Gen-Z wellness.
- Pilates-girl culture.
- Beauty brand confidence.
- Fashion/editorial energy.
- Internet-native.
- Playful.
- Clean but not sterile.
- Premium but attainable.
- Feminine without becoming cliché.
- Strong without becoming aggressive.

## What bodies is NOT
Do not make it feel:
- Like a commercial gym.
- Like a hardcore bodybuilding company.
- Like an old-school Pilates studio.
- Like a medical/clinical fitness company.
- Like generic “girlboss” wellness.
- Like an ultra-expensive luxury fitness machine.
- Like a beige minimalist wellness brand with no personality.

## Messaging territory
Strong themes:
- Pilates at home.
- A stronger, happier you.
- Studio feeling, different location.
- No commute.
- Small space.
- One board, many ways to move.
- Sculpt.
- Tone.
- Feel stronger.
- Movement as part of everyday life.
- Beautiful enough to live in your home.

Possible copy directions:
- “PILATES AT HOME.”
- “A STRONGER, HAPPIER YOU.”
- “NO GYM. NO LIMITS.”
- “MORE THAN A WORKOUT.”
- “ONE BOARD. SO MANY WAYS TO MOVE.”
- “WORKOUTS THAT MOVE WITH YOU.”
- “SMALL SPACE. BIG CHANGE.”
- “JOIN THE MOVEMENT.”

Do not treat these as locked taglines; they are established creative territory.

## Audience
Primary visual/audience archetype:
**The aesthetic Instagram Pilates girl.**

She likely values:
- Reformer Pilates.
- Sculpting/tone.
- Core/glutes/legs/arms.
- Low-impact movement.
- Stretching and recovery.
- Cute workout sets.
- Beautiful wellness products.
- Routine.
- Convenience.
- Home aesthetics.
- Instagram/TikTok culture.
- Feeling good and looking stronger rather than hardcore athletic performance.

## Emotional promise
The product should make the customer feel:
“I have my own Pilates studio at home.”

The deeper promise:
“I can build a movement routine that fits my life and my space.”
