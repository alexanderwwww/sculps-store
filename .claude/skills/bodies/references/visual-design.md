# BODIES VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM

## Core visual reference
The strongest reference is the uploaded **Experiment Beauty** website.

The goal is not to copy its exact content. The goal is to translate its visual intelligence into fitness.

## Design principle
**Clean structure + aggressive color + editorial photography + lots of breathing room.**

This combination is more important than any single font, color, or section.

## Layout
Use:
- Generous white space.
- Strong section separation.
- Large visual blocks.
- Clean grids.
- Product cards with breathing room.
- Asymmetrical editorial moments where useful.
- Occasional oversized type.
- Short copy blocks.
- Strong CTAs.
- Minimal navigation.

Avoid:
- Filling every pixel.
- Overloading the page with gradients.
- Making every section colorful.
- Making every section identical.
- Generic SaaS layouts.

## Color strategy
Color should be a major part of the identity.

Primary visual families:
- Neon lime.
- Icy cyan/blue.
- Pastel pink/lilac.
- Deep green.
- Neon chartreuse.
- Black.
- White.

Use color in:
- Announcement bars.
- Product cards.
- Hero photography.
- Product styling.
- CTA buttons.
- Full-width campaign banners.
- Handwritten annotations.
- Newsletter sections.

The key is **contrast**:
white → saturated color → white → photographic color → white → neon.

## Typography
Use bold, chunky, modern sans-serif typography.

Characteristics:
- Heavy display headlines.
- Short lines.
- High contrast between headline and body.
- Black typography on white.
- Occasional oversized words.
- Simple functional navigation.

The typography should feel closer to a modern beauty/fashion ecommerce brand than a traditional fitness company.

## Handwritten elements
Use handwritten annotations sparingly but confidently.

Examples:
- “SAME STUDIO FEELING. DIFFERENT LOCATION.”
- “PILATES FITS HERE TOO.”
- “SMALL SPACE. BIG CHANGE.”
- “REAL MOVEMENT. REAL PROGRESS.”

They should feel like editorial markup, not childish doodles.

## Photography

**The weapon of this brand is relatability, not polish.**

Every image with a person in it is shot the way she shoots herself: iPhone, raw,
unedited. Imperfect light. Slightly off framing. A real room with real things in
it. No retouching, no colour grade, no studio lighting, no art director.

She is on Instagram all day. She photographs herself all day. She knows exactly
what a paid campaign looks like and she scrolls past it. What stops her is
something that looks like it came from someone she follows.

### The rule
- **Anything with a human in it:** iPhone RAW. Amateur. Unedited.
- **Anything that is only the product:** clean render. Sharp, lit, correct.

That contrast is the whole point. A crisp product shot next to a raw phone photo
reads as a real brand with real customers. Making *everything* raw reads as a
dropshipper who could not afford photography.

### Woman
- Real, not model-perfect. Mid-twenties to late thirties.
- Ordinary good looks. Visible skin texture. No retouching.
- Hair not done. Sometimes mid-workout messy.
- Contemporary workout sets, but worn-in, not a styled outfit.
- Caught mid-movement or mid-nothing, never posing for a camera.

### How the shot should read
- Taken on a phone propped against something, or held by a friend.
- Window light only. Blown highlights and dark corners are fine and wanted.
- Slight motion blur, slight tilt, autofocus hunting: all fine.
- A cup on the floor, a laundry pile just out of frame, a normal apartment.
- Mirror selfies, with the phone visible in the mirror.

### Locations
Real homes. Small apartments. A bedroom with the bed unmade. A living room with
the sofa pushed back to make space. Sunlight through a normal window. Never a
studio, never a set, never a resort.

### Never
- Editorial campaign lighting on a person.
- Retouched skin.
- A model who looks hired.
- Colour grading or filters.
- Stock fitness photography of any kind.

## Product photography
The machine should be the hero when the section is product-focused.

Use:
- Strong angles.
- Close-ups.
- Color-specific environments.
- Clean shadows.
- Realistic materials.
- Premium consumer-electronics finish.

Avoid:
- Fake sci-fi CGI.
- Excessive metallic luxury.
- Commercial gym aesthetics.

## Website rhythm
A strong page should breathe.

A good rhythm:
1. Neon announcement bar.
2. Large hero.
3. White product collection section.
4. White/photographic brand story.
5. White workout grid.
6. App/product feature section.
7. Saturated product lifestyle banner.
8. White testimonials.
9. Saturated newsletter/closing campaign.
10. Clean footer.

Not every section needs a colored background.

## Gen-Z rule
Do not confuse Gen-Z with “make everything neon.”

Gen-Z energy comes from:
- Cultural styling.
- Photography.
- Unexpected combinations.
- Humor/playfulness.
- Strong typography.
- Confident copy.
- Internet-native visual cues.
- Real-looking people.
- Color used intentionally.
