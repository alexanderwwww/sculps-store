# The Scout

flip's eyes. Not "look at ten listings and have a feeling" — that is what a
person does, and it is the thing a machine should never imitate.

## The advantage, stated plainly

A human seller opens twenty tabs, eyeballs them, and picks. That is the method
in every tutorial, and it is the ceiling of the whole field.

flip can read **every listing in a category** — five hundred, a thousand — and
count. Not faster guessing: arithmetic where everyone else has intuition.

That means the Scout can answer questions no human on Depop can:

- Of every tote bag listed in the last 30 days, **which price band actually
  converts** — not which is most listed, which gets into bags.
- Which **words in a title** appear disproportionately in the ones that sold.
- Which **colourway** of the same item moves and which sits.
- Which **aesthetic** is rising week over week, before it is obvious.
- Which sellers are **actually** selling versus which just list a lot.

That is the entire edge. Everything below is how to get it.

## The signals, ranked

**Money moving, in order of how much it proves:**

1. **In N people's bags** — someone put it in a basket. The strongest signal on
   the platform and the one the tutorials all converge on.
2. **Offers sent** — someone wanted it enough to negotiate.
3. **Sold, on the seller's profile** — with the review count beside it. A high
   sold-to-review ratio means real, repeated trade.
4. **Likes** — interest, not intent. Useful only as a tiebreak, and never on
   its own. A listing with 26 likes and zero bags is dead.

**What each raw number has to be divided by before it means anything:**

- **By age.** 4 bags in three days is a different animal from 4 bags in three
  months. Depop does not print the listing date on a card, so the Scout has to
  open the listing or remember when it first saw it. **Velocity, not totals** —
  this is the single correction that separates the Scout from the tutorials.
- **By the seller's reach.** 10 offers on a shop with 20k followers is weak. On
  a shop with 200, it is a product doing the work.
- **By price.** Demand at €12 and demand at €120 are different businesses.

**Read it in bulk, always.** One listing is an anecdote. The pattern across two
hundred is the product.

## The aesthetic vocabulary

Depop's buyers are mostly young women, and they do not shop by garment, they
shop by **aesthetic**. The word is the search term, the hashtag, and the
identity. Getting the vocabulary right is most of getting found.

The live set, with what actually sells inside each:

| Aesthetic | Reads as | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Y2K** | 2000s, low-rise, shiny, playful | baby tees, cargo, micro bags, butterfly anything, tinted sunglasses |
| **coquette** | bows, lace, pink, soft | ribbon detail, mary janes, cardigans, slip dresses |
| **downtown / indie sleaze** | messy, 2008, flash photo | band tees, leather, skinny scarves, ballet flats |
| **dark academia** | tweed, libraries, autumn | blazers, pleated skirts, loafers, signet rings |
| **grunge / emo** | black, studs, 2000s alt | band merch, plaid, chunky boots, chokers |
| **gorpcore** | outdoors worn in the city | fleeces, shells, Salomon-adjacent, Carhartt |
| **clean girl / minimal** | quiet, beige, gold | slip skirts, fine jewellery, plain knits, totes |
| **streetwear** | hype-adjacent | graphic tees, workwear, technical outer |

Two rules that come straight from the method: **pick the person, not the
niche** — an aesthetic is a person, a season is not — and **one aesthetic per
shop** until it is proven, because a page that spans four of these reads as a
random thrift shop and converts like one.

The vocabulary shifts. It is a file to update, not a truth.

## Where the Scout looks

**On Depop, in this order:**

1. **Category + brand "Other"** — where the unbranded goods live, which is where
   the dropshippers are and therefore where the demand is measurable.
2. **A price floor** (~€20) to strip out genuine secondhand clutter.
3. **Aesthetic terms as searches**, not just garment names. "coquette top" and
   "white top" are different markets.
4. **Winning sellers' whole shops** — when one listing performs, read the rest
   of that seller. The shape of their page is a free experiment someone else
   paid for.
5. **His own sold items.** The most valuable data he owns and the one nobody
   else can copy. What sold, at what price, how long it sat, who bought it.

**Off Depop, for what is about to happen rather than what already has:**

- **TikTok and Reels** — the aesthetic names appear here weeks before they are
  Depop search terms. This is the leading indicator.
- **Pinterest** — what gets saved is what gets searched for next season.
- **Vinted, Grailed, eBay sold listings** — cross-check that demand is real and
  not a Depop-only bubble, and sanity-check price.
- **AliExpress order counts** — a crude but honest read on what is shipping at
  volume.

## Turning a read into a hunt list

The Scout's output is never "this is trending." It is a row he can act on:

```
carhartt detroit jacket · brown · gorpcore
  demand    14 bags / 31 offers across 22 live listings, 30 days
  velocity  rising — 6 of those bagged in the last 7 days
  price     converts at €85–110; below €70 sits, above €130 sits
  source    €38 · verified supplier · 14 day ship
  spread    €52 at the middle of the band
  why       every one that sold said "detroit" and "brown" in the title;
            the black ones sit
```

Four things every row must carry: **proof it sells**, **the price band that
converts**, **what it costs to get**, and **the one sentence that explains it.**
A recommendation without the sentence is a guess wearing a number.

## Reading his own shop back to him

Once there is sales history, the Scout stops guessing and starts reporting:

- Cluster what sold by aesthetic, garment, colour and price band.
- Say what the shop actually *is*, in one line — "your emo pieces sell, your
  hats don't" is the method's own example and it is the right altitude.
- Name the dead stock, with how long it has sat and what it is costing.
- Hold the €1k/day target as arithmetic: at his real average sale and margin,
  how many listings a day that needs, and whether the shop is short.

## Pace

Reading is browsing, and browsing is free — but the Scout reads far more than a
person would, and rhythm is what gets accounts flagged. So: human gaps between
pages, no bursts, and the reading budget is separate from the acting budget.
A shop that gets banned for looking at things is the stupidest possible way to
lose this.

## What the Scout is not allowed to do

- Recommend sourcing a **replica**, ever, whatever the margin looks like. He
  sources genuine at every level, which is the shop's entire advantage over
  every seller it is reading.
- Repost a competitor's **photograph or description**. Their image is
  reference input for generating his own — which is automatic and no slower —
  never the file that gets uploaded. Depop's duplicate detection is the flag
  that bans accounts.
- Present a number it did not read. If it could not get the listing age, it says
  "age unknown" — it does not invent a velocity.
