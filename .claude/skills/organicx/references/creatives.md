# Creatives

Everything here rests on one rule: **nothing is invented from your own head.**
Every change is an iteration on something already proven.

## The elements of a clip

Any video breaks into these. Learn to see them without AI — AI is an extension
of you, and if you cannot name a hook it cannot either.

| Element | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The first seconds. The reason anyone stays. |
| **Main body** | The demonstration. |
| **Call to action** | What they should do. |
| **Script** | What is said. |
| **Sound / voice** | Who says it and how. |
| **Subtitles** | The on-screen text. |
| **Music** | The bed. |
| **Transitions** | How it is cut. |

A clip went viral because of **one of these, or the mix**. Change something
blindly and you may remove the exact thing that worked. That is why changes are
small and evidence-led.

## Posting order

1. **Post the viral creative exactly as it is, first.** Unchanged. This is the
   single most-skipped step and the most common reason people get no sales —
   they grab random footage and post that instead.
2. Post the rest of the viral creatives you found.
3. Then start iterating.

## Iterations — small, evidence-led

From one clip you can make a hundred. Each change comes from something observed
working elsewhere in the niche:

- **Swap the hook** — for one seen going viral on a similar product
- **Restamp the subtitles** — different wording, different colour, different
  position
- **Stamp a big text hook over the first seconds** and let it disappear
- **Reorder** — move the payoff to the front, cut the hook to the end
- **New music** — one currently going viral in that niche
- **New voice** — strip the audio and clone a voice saying your script
- **New script** — built from patterns across competitors' viral clips

Mechanically: mute the original, extract the voice track, slap it back over the
recut, regenerate captions.

Do this until something outperforms. Then find out why, and iterate on *that*.
It is a loop, not a task.

## Variations — bigger, optional

Cloning a UGC clip with a different actor, AI-regenerated scenes, and similar.
Costs money and time, and **is not needed** to make five figures organically.
Often a waste. Play with iterations first.

## Self-copy

The most underused move: when a video outperforms your others, **repost the
exact same video again**. Not a variation. The same one.

The pattern is real — 2k becomes 5k, 5k becomes 10k, sometimes it goes properly
viral on the third or fourth posting. The algorithm did not see it the first
time.

## Research that feeds iterations

1. **Avatar research** — who the buyer is. Age, concerns, problems, hobbies.
2. From the avatar, **find the competitors** — through keywords, across TikTok,
   Instagram, YouTube and the software.
3. Build a **spreadsheet**: every competitor, every viral clip, its hook, its
   body, its CTA, its script, its text, its music.
4. Feed the avatar research *and* the spreadsheet *and* your current viral
   creative to an LLM, and ask for ten hooks to iterate with.

The result is avatar-specific rather than generic. That is the difference
between "give me variations" and a targeted iteration.

## AI's real role

Useful. Not magic. It is an extension of your own judgement — if you are a
beginner, it produces beginner work and you cannot tell.

| Tool | Job |
|---|---|
| Gemini | **Write the prompts.** Talk to it for five minutes explaining the task like it is a child, then ask it to produce a copy-pasteable prompt. |
| Claude | Copywriting, hook iterations, competitor analysis, deep research |
| ChatGPT | Product images — *"remember this photo"*, then recompose with the real product |
| ElevenLabs | Voice cloning for new scripts over an existing clip |
| Google Flow | Video from a reference image, free tier is enough |
| Fireflies | Transcribe a competitor's clip to get its exact script |
| CapCut | The actual editing |

**What AI must never do here: fabricate the product.** Generated imagery is for
scenes and atmosphere, with the real product as reference. Anything claiming to
show the product itself must be a real photograph.
