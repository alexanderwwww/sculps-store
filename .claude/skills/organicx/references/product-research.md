# Product research

90% of the time goes here. This is the whole competitive edge.

Run several methods, not one. Someone running ten methods finds at least one
winner a day and has spare winners to hand out. Someone running one method
complains that the method is dead.

## 1. Burners — and how they are actually done

A burner is an account you feed until its algorithm shows you nothing but
products worth running. Most people do this wrong in two specific ways.

**Mistake one: one account.** One burner shows you the same products forever.
The fix is many: three accounts each on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Facebook
— twelve burners — and each one feeds differently and shows you different
things. This single change solves the "my burner shows me nothing new" complaint
that everybody has.

**Mistake two: feeding it rubbish.** You cannot feed a burner correctly unless
you already know what a validated product looks like. The average person taps
into every mediocre dropshipping product they see, and trains the algorithm to
serve them mediocre products forever.

### The sequence

Run the **full sequence only on genuinely good products**:

1. Like
2. Comment
3. Save
4. Share
5. Open the store
6. Add to cart
7. Initiate checkout
8. *(optional)* Attempt the purchase with a card that has no funds or is frozen

Step 8 registers as a purchase event without money moving, which makes the
algorithm treat the account as a buyer. It works especially well on Facebook and
Instagram.

On products that are merely **okay**: a bare like. Nothing else.

That contrast is the whole trick. Feed the full sequence to winners only and
within weeks the feed is almost pure winners.

### Validate from a different account

Never validate a product from the burner you are feeding. Searching competitors
and keywords pollutes the algorithm you spent weeks training. **Keep one
dedicated burner whose only job is validation.**

## 2. The keyword rabbit hole

On the phone (or a mobile emulator on the laptop — the filters do not exist on
desktop).

1. Search a generic seed: `TikTok made me buy it`, `Amazon finds`, `free
   shipping`, and seasonal terms.
2. **Filter to recent.** Anything older than a month is finished. Sort by views,
   not likes.
3. When a product appears, **steal its keywords** — the specific words in its
   caption and on screen. Add them to a running list.
4. Search one of those keywords. Go deeper. Steal again. Repeat.

Each hop narrows toward terms nobody else is searching. The list compounds and
becomes an asset in itself.

## 3. Software

Kalodata, PPSPY, Adspy, Ads Paro, Afterlib, Meta Ad Library.

People who say software is pointless are usually people who cannot use it. It
costs real money every month for a reason.

The filters that matter for organic:

- **Recent.** Last 7 days, or last 30 at the widest.
- **Modest revenue floors.** Not 100k. A thousand or two in sales per video. You
  are looking for things that are working and *not yet saturated*.
- **Revenue trend: growing.**
- **Videos, not ads.** UGC-shaped content you can actually clip.
- Skip anything that is clearly a whole appliance or otherwise unfilmable.

There are two species of product: those already running in the Meta Ad Library,
and those not. For organic you want the second kind. Do not hunt organic winners
in an ad library — that is where you find things already being scaled with money.

A trick worth knowing: widen the date range to a year and look for **seasonal
spikes**. Something that spiked last October and is spiking again now is a
validated seasonal product.

## 4. Manual — Temu and AliExpress

Needs experience; a beginner cannot tell a dead product from a live one here.

Go to categories, sort by top sales, and look for three things at once:

1. **You have not seen it before**
2. **It makes you say "what is that"** — genuine wow, gimmick is fine if it lands
3. **It has a use** — it does something

When something qualifies, **validate it first**, then add to cart — which feeds
that platform's algorithm too.

### The calendar trick

Give an LLM the full category list and ask it to build a **seasonal research
calendar** — which categories to research in which week of which month. Then you
are researching Halloween categories in late August, two months ahead, instead
of the week everybody else is.

## Output

The realistic target for someone running burners, keywords and one software:
**one good product a week.** Four a month. Someone running all the methods
properly finds two or three a week.
