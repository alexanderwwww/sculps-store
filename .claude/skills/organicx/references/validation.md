# Validation

Finding a product without validating it is the same as not finding it.

And validation is **not** "it solves a problem and it has a wow factor". Lovely.
Everyone says that. It means nothing on its own.

## What counts as viral

People throw the word around. It has a shape:

| Numbers | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 100k in a day | viral |
| 1M in two days | viral |
| 3M in three days | viral |
| 10–20k in a day | **not viral** |
| 500k in a month | **not viral** |

Views over time, not raw views.

## Recency

**7–14 days is the target. 30 days is the outer limit.**

Anything older is finished — by the time you have made content, a hundred people
have run it. On a research feed, always filter to recent.

## Competitors

| Method | Limit |
|---|---|
| **Copy method** | Under 5. Two or three is the sweet spot. |
| **Classic organic** (you order and film it) | **Two or three maximum** |

The classic-organic limit is stricter for a physical reason: by the time the
product reaches Greece, two to three weeks have passed and more competitors have
arrived. If it already has four, you have lost before you start.

If you have found something good, more people will enter while you are making
content. That is why the ceiling is five and the target is two.

**Express shipping (UPS/FedEx) is worth the customs hassle** if you are doing
classic organic from Greece. It is the only way to be early. A US competitor
sees the product and has it the next day.

## The full list — copy method

1. **Not saturated.** Unless there is a genuinely new angle.
2. **Under 5 competitors**, ideally 2–3.
3. **A viral creative**, recent — proof of concept.
4. **Problem solving.**
5. **Wow factor.**
6. **Viral because of the product, not because of controversy.** See below.
7. **Content exists on the internet** to cut and stitch. With only one clip you
   are limited; with a library you can play.

## The full list — classic organic

1. **Proof of concept** — at least one viral creative.
2. **Two or three competitors maximum.**
3. **Wow factor**, weighted heavily.
4. **Controversy potential** — can you immediately picture two or three concepts?
5. **Can you even film it?** If it needs a space you do not have, a set you would
   have to rent, or a season that is not now, it is not your product yet.

## The controversy trap

A clip can go viral for reasons that have nothing to do with the product —
something shocking in frame, something absurd, someone behaving ridiculously.
Those get views and **do not get sales**. People watched the joke.

How to tell: **read the comments.** Are they laughing, or are they asking *where
do I buy this*?

For the copy method especially, a product that converts is worth more than a
product that trends. Views you cannot convert cost you time and teach you
nothing.

Controversy is not useless — a bit of it early can help an account catch. Just
do not mistake it for product validation.

## Do not over-philosophise

A hundred people will watch the same course and find reasons every product is
wrong. Do not be one of them.

If something has **two validations**, run it. It is organic — it costs nothing
but time. Seven competitors instead of five is not a reason to skip. Not testing
anything is the only real failure.

Judgement, not superstition: weigh the signals, decide how much risk you want,
and move.
