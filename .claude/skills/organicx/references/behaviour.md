# Human behaviour and the persona

An account that only posts is a billboard, and the platforms know it. An account
that watches, likes, saves, follows, comments and occasionally does nothing for
a day is a person.

**The behaviour is not decoration around the posting. It is the thing that makes
the posting work.** Warming, reach and the "recommended to the right people"
effect all come from what the account does when it is not posting.

## The persona

Every account gets one, written once and then kept. It is stored with the
account and never regenerated, because a person does not change who they are
between Tuesday and Wednesday.

A persona holds:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| **Who they are** | 34, suburban, two kids, owns the house, decorates hard for Halloween |
| **Where** | A real US metro, consistent with the VPN exit |
| **Waking hours** | Up at 6:40, phone in hand at 7:05, again at lunch, properly in the evening |
| **Interests beyond the product** | Home improvement, dogs, high-school football, deals and coupons |
| **Voice** | Short. Lowercase. Uses "omg" and "no way". Never uses a semicolon. |
| **Typing** | Speed, and the rate at which they typo and do not fix it |
| **Engagement temperament** | Liker, not a commenter. Or a serial commenter who rarely follows. |
| **Days off** | Everyone has them. Some weeks are quiet. |

The persona drives every decision below. Two accounts on the same product must
not behave identically — if they do, they are one bot with two names.

## Session rhythm

Not a cron tick. A person picks the phone up.

- **Sessions, not uptime.** Three to six short sessions a day, at that persona's
  hours, with real variance. Some are ninety seconds. One is twenty minutes on
  the sofa.
- **Never the same times two days running.**
- **Some days are thin.** One session, two likes. Some days are nothing at all.
- **Ramp up.** A brand-new account does far less in week one than in week four.

## Scrolling

- Watch times vary and follow interest, not a script. Some clips to the end,
  some abandoned in half a second, a few rewatched.
- **Occasionally stop and do nothing** — the thumb rests, the video loops twice.
- Scroll speed varies inside a session. People slow down when they get absorbed.
- Go into a profile sometimes. Look at a grid. Leave without doing anything.
- Follow the search bar somewhere on a whim, then come back to the feed.

## Engagement, as ratios

A real person likes a small fraction of what they see. Ratios per persona,
roughly:

| Action | Rough rate |
|---|---|
| **Like** | 3–8% of clips watched |
| **Save** | rare — a handful a week |
| **Share** | rarer — and to nobody, just the button |
| **Comment** | a few a week, not a few an hour |
| **Follow** | a few a week, clustered — people binge-follow |

Skewed toward the niche, but **not only the niche**. An account that has only
ever touched Halloween content is not a person, it is a campaign. The persona's
other interests have to show up in the feed history.

## Commenting

The highest-risk behaviour and the one that most looks like a bot when done
badly.

- **In the persona's voice**, every time. Short, specific to what is actually in
  the clip, lowercase if that is who they are.
- **Never generic.** "Love this!" and "So cool 🔥" are the signature of a bot.
- Reference the actual content — the dog, the house, the timing of the jump.
- **Typos happen and are not corrected.**
- Reply to a reply occasionally. Conversations are what humans have.
- Never comment your own product under someone else's video. That is spam and it
  is how accounts get reported rather than recommended.

## Following

- Clustered, not evenly spread — people follow six accounts in one sitting and
  none for four days.
- The niche, plus the persona's other interests.
- Follow back sometimes.
- Unfollow occasionally. Real follow lists churn.

## Typing

Anywhere text is entered — a comment, a caption, a search:

- Human speed with variance, not instant paste.
- Pauses mid-sentence.
- Occasional backspace and retype.
- Occasional typo left standing.

## Posting is one behaviour among many

The posting cadence in `accounts.md` still holds. What changes is that it sits
inside a day of other activity rather than being the only thing the account
ever does.

An account whose entire history is "posted, posted, posted" reads exactly as
what it is.

## Hard limits

- **Never comment on, follow, or engage with a direct competitor's post** in a
  way that draws attention to the account.
- **Never mass-follow or mass-like.** Bursts are the clearest automation tell
  there is.
- **Never run two personas with the same rhythm** on the same device.
- **Never post the same asset twice to the same account** (self-copy means
  reposting a file that performed — which is a deliberate, spaced repeat, not a
  duplicate in the same week).
- **Stop on friction.** A captcha, a verification prompt, an action-blocked
  notice, an unusual-login warning — stop that account for the day and report
  it. Pushing through is how accounts are lost.
