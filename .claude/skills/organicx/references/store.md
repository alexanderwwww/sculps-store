# The store

The store matters far less than people think. Sellers have scaled with genuinely
ugly stores. What it must be is *presentable* and *working* — not beautiful.

## Ten minutes, not two hours

The common failure is spending hours perfecting a store for a product that gets
cut in five days. Do the reverse:

1. Build the bare minimum. Ten minutes.
2. Launch the product. Test it.
3. Dead in five days? Next product, nothing lost.
4. Getting sales? **Then** spend half an hour making it good.

## Minimum viable

- **A generic domain.** Something that does not lock you to one niche, since you
  will change products constantly. Roughly $10–16. Nobody has ever failed
  because of a domain name.
- **Product page** with real photographs — not AliExpress descriptions, not a
  wall of black-and-white images.
- **Bundles.** The one thing worth having from day one.
- **Free shipping**, built into the price. Never a shipping charge — never a gate
  between cart and payment. $29.99 free beats $24.99 plus $5.
- **Optional paid extras** at checkout — shipping protection, priority handling.
  Small yeses.
- **Policy pages** — contact, privacy, returns, terms.
- **Test the checkout yourself.** One real payment through. Do not launch on the
  assumption it works.

## Copying structure

Find a competitor whose store is good and copy the *structure*. Run the text
through an LLM to rewrite it. Replace every photograph with your own product.
Take reference images and recompose them with your product through AI.

Nobody reads the fine print. What they look at is the photographs, the price and
the offer.

## Suppliers — later

Do not hunt a supplier before you have sales. It is the classic way to spend a
week building a "business" and never test anything.

Instead: ask an LLM to deep-research the typical wholesale price for the product
across AliExpress, Alibaba, Taobao and Amazon, tell it your target margin, and
let it give you a price to list at. Set that price, run the product, and **only
once sales come in** go and find a real supplier.

Once volume arrives, a proper fulfilment partner is worth paying for — placing a
hundred orders a day by hand is time stolen from research, and it is a worse
experience for the customer. The same goes for customer support: answering 500
emails a morning is time that should have gone into finding the next product.
