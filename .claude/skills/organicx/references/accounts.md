# Accounts, phones and posting

## Phone setup

An iPhone is easiest. An old one is fine — older ones are often better.

1. **Factory reset.**
2. **Apple ID with everything set to the US.** Pick a state. **Write down every
   detail you used** — you will need them again and most people do not record them.
3. A **US phone number** is needed once, for the Apple ID verification SMS. A
   cheap number service for a couple of euros does it; cancel immediately after.
4. **Insert a SIM.** Do not activate it, do not configure it. The phone just has
   one in it. A Greek SIM is fine.
5. **VPN, always on, never turned off.** NordVPN or Surfshark both work. A
   dedicated IP is not necessary — it works either way.
6. Install the apps and go.

That is all of it. The SIM and the VPN are not what makes a product fail. The
video and the product are.

## Warming

Depends on the platform:

| Platform | Warming |
|---|---|
| **TikTok** | **Warm it.** Skipping it can work, but bans happen — it has been tested and they happen. |
| **Instagram** | Not necessary in 2026. |
| **YouTube** | Standard warming. Ten to fifteen minutes of scrolling on day one, using product-related terms so it starts feeding you products immediately. |

**Make the account you post from a burner too.** Every account feeds differently,
so five products means five accounts means five feeds showing five different
sets of products. That is how you solve the "my burner shows me the same things"
problem for free.

## Primed Instagram accounts

The single highest-leverage thing in organic, and almost nobody talks about it.

A **primed account** is one that has reached the point where even a mediocre
video gets views — consistently above 10,000 on anything posted. It makes money
almost by itself.

How to build one:

1. Make the account. Do not warm it.
2. **Day one: repost viral videos from your niche**, exactly as they are. Do not
   touch the caption, do not touch the hashtags. Download on the phone, upload
   from the same phone.
3. Do that for a day or two. There is a real chance the account catches
   immediately.
4. Then start posting the product.
5. **Watch for the outlier.** If everything sits at 300 views and one hits 1,000
   — or everything sits at 1k and one hits 3k — that is the signal.
6. **Repost that exact video. Again and again.** 2k becomes 5k, 5k becomes 10k.
   The same file.

Most people miss step five entirely. They post a week of 1k videos, one hits 4k,
and they ignore it and carry on posting randomly.

## One product per account

One product, one account. Do not run two products on the same account.

When a product dies after five days, **do not delete the clips and do not delete
the account** — it is already warm. Replace the product and keep going.

Multiple devices means multiple products in parallel. Five products a week
across five accounts beats one product a month, and that is the whole edge.

## Posting cadence

| Platform | Cadence |
|---|---|
| **TikTok** | 2 a day, ~3 hours apart. More is risky. |
| **YouTube** | 1–2 a day. **Never skip a day** — one today and one tomorrow beats two today and none tomorrow. |
| **Instagram** | 1–3 a day is safe. Up to 10 is possible and passes fine, if the product is quick enough to film. |

Find the **hours that work for your audience** and post at them. For a US
audience from Europe this matters a lot. Test, observe, then keep to it.

Of two or three posts a day: two are iterations of what is already working, one
is a test of something new seen in the niche.

## Equipment — classic organic only

- **Two lights.** Cheap ones from AliExpress. Not a ring light — proper lights.
- **A tripod.** The cheapest one.
- **A chest mount** — magnetic, holds the phone at chest height for first-person
  shots with both hands free. More products need this than you would think.
- **Clean the lens before every shoot.** A fingerprint ruins the whole clip and
  nobody notices until it is posted.
- **Backdrop paper.** Changing the background alone has taken videos from 1k to
  15k — same movements, same angle, same everything else.

## Product tagging

On Instagram, Facebook and YouTube you can **tag the product in the reel** —
the viewer taps the image and lands directly in the store. Almost nobody does
this on YouTube. It removes the profile → bio → link → product journey entirely
and it visibly lifts conversion.

## Checking whether a competitor actually converts

Going viral is not the same as selling.

If their store has an optional paid extra at checkout (shipping protection,
priority handling), **place one small order, then place another the next day.**
Subtract the two order numbers and you know exactly how many orders they took in
between.

Worth knowing in reverse: on your own store you can change the order-number
format so nobody can do this to you.
