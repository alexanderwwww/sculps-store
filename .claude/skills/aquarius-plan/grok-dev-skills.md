# Grok Forge skills pack: creative coin ideas and good images (Alex, 2026-11-16: "he can't find creative ideas and make nice images, so tell him")
Paste under the Grok Forge prompt (or send as a message to the bot).

---
SKILLS PACK FOR GROK FORGE. Use these every time. Never hand Alex a concept that skipped a step.

SKILL 1: FIND THE FEELING (before any name)
A coin that lasts is a feeling people already have, given a face. Every day, search X for:
- phrases repeated word for word by many unrelated accounts in the last 48 hours (a running joke, a complaint, a mood);
- images people keep reposting with new captions;
- feelings with no coin yet, or only lazy copies (search the phrase + "CA", "pump.fun", "$").
Write each one as: "People feel ___ about ___, and they say it like this: '___'." Collect 20, keep the 5 where the feeling is strong, shared by normal people (not only crypto accounts) and will still be true in three months. A one-day news event is not a feeling.

SKILL 2: TURN A FEELING INTO A CHARACTER
For each feeling, invent ONE character who lives it. Fill this card:
- Who is it (animal, object, creature; never a real person or an existing character)?
- What does it want, and what always goes wrong?
- Its one face expression (the thing people will screenshot).
- Its catchphrase (5 words or fewer, sayable out loud).
- Three situations it can be dropped into forever (this is what makes memes easy for holders to make).
Test: could a stranger draw it from memory after seeing it once? Could they caption it without knowing crypto? If no, redo it.

SKILL 3: NAME AND TICKER
- 1 or 2 words, easy to say in any language, fun to type. Ticker 3 to 5 letters.
- Make 30 names, cut to 5, then check each: search X, pump.fun, DexScreener and Google for the name and the ticker. Drop anything already used by a coin, a company, a game or a known person.
- Say the name out loud in a sentence a holder would post. If it sounds forced, drop it.

SKILL 4: THE STORY (lore)
Write 5 lines: where the character came from, what it wants, who its enemy is (a feeling, never a real person or project), what happens when the community grows, and one mystery left open. The 90-day plan is the story continuing: one new chapter a week (an image, a short clip, a tiny game), each one something you can really ship.

SKILL 5: ART DIRECTION (decide before generating anything)
Write a one-page style sheet and keep it for every image:
- Style: pick ONE (flat sticker with thick outline / 90s cartoon cel / clay 3D toy / pixel art / ink doodle). Never mix.
- Palette: 3 colours plus black and white, written as names and hex codes.
- The character's fixed features: body shape, eye shape, one signature item (hat, scar, scarf), proportions (for example "head = half the body").
- What it never has (no realistic fur, no extra limbs, no text on the body).

SKILL 6: IMAGE PROMPTS THAT COME OUT GOOD
Always generate in this order and reuse the first approved image as the reference for all the others:
1. MASTER SHEET: "Character design sheet of [character], [style], [palette], front view, side view, back view, 4 face expressions, plain white background, clean thick outlines, no text."
2. LOGO: "Centered head-and-shoulders icon of [character] from the sheet, same style, bold simple shapes, readable at 64 pixels, solid [colour] circle background, no text."
3. BANNER (3:1): "[character] on the left third, [simple scene from the lore], empty space on the right for a title, same style and palette."
4. MEME TEMPLATES (make 6): the character in each of its three situations, with an empty area for a caption, same style.
5. STICKERS (make 8): one emotion each, transparent background, thick white outline.
Prompt rules: describe the subject first, then the action, then the style, then the background, then what to avoid. One subject per image. Name the camera angle. Say "consistent with the reference image" every time. Never ask for text inside the image: add the words afterwards.
Check every image before showing Alex: same character as the sheet? right number of fingers, eyes, limbs? readable when tiny? no stray letters or logos? no likeness of a real person or a known character? Regenerate what fails; show only the best 3.

SKILL 7: THE CONCEPT PAGE FOR ALEX
One screen per concept: the feeling (with 3 real X posts as proof, labelled CLAIMED), the character card, name and ticker with the "not already used" check, the 5-line story, the style sheet, the logo and one meme, the 90-day chapters, the launch cost, the risks. Bring 3 concepts, ranked, and say which one you would launch and why.

SKILL 8: LEARN FROM WHAT LASTED
Each week study 3 memecoins that kept a community for 6 months or more and 3 that died in a week. For each: what was the feeling, the character, the art style, how the team behaved. Write 3 lessons and apply one to your next concept.

The fair-launch rules in your main instructions always win over everything here.
