## 2. Video with FAQ

**Heading:** Watch it work.

**Subheading:** The questions people ask before they buy one. Answered straight.

**Q. How long does it take?**
A. We are not publishing a time until we have measured it on the production machine. The
number you see on a competitor's page was written by a marketing team, not a
thermometer. As soon as our own testing is done, the measured time goes on this page and
on the box. If a number matters to you that much, wait for it — we would rather lose the
sale than lie to you about it.

**Q. Do I need a special bottle?**
A. No. A standard sealed plastic or aluminium bottle lies on the rollers. That's the
whole point: cryo chills the water you already bought, or the one you filled at the tap.

**Q. Does it need a fridge or a freezer?**
A. You need a freezer to refreeze the cartridge, the same way you need one for an ice
pack. You do not need a fridge anywhere near cryo while it is working — that is what
makes it work in an office, a dorm room, a garage, an RV or a hotel.

**Q. How many bottles per cartridge?**
A. Roughly 3 to 4 in a row before the cartridge needs refreezing. Exact count is being
measured on the sample and goes in the specs when it is.

**Q. Will it freeze my water solid?**
A. No, and that is why the bottle spins. Spinning keeps the water moving so it chills
evenly instead of forming ice on the inside wall. The infrared sensor ends the cycle at
your set temperature.

**Q. Does it plug into the wall?**
A. It charges over USB-C, from any phone charger, laptop, or power bank, in the US or in
Europe. It runs off its internal battery, so it does not have to stay plugged in while
it works.

**Q. Can I chill soda, beer, or a can?**
A. Yes — anything sealed that fits on the rollers. Do not put an open container in it.

**Q. How big is it?**
A. About 28 × 14 × 14 cm — roughly a lunchbox lying on its side. It lives on a counter
without taking the counter.

**Q. Is it loud?**
A. There is a small motor and a pump, so it is not silent. We are not putting a decibel
number on this page until we have measured one.

**Q. What if I don't like it?**
A. 30 days. Send it back, get your money back.

## 9. Features

**Heading:** What it actually is.

**Subheading:** Every line here is a thing you can see in the pictures.

- **Two-roller spin drive.** The bottle lies on its side and turns. Moving water chills
  evenly instead of icing up against the wall.
- **Sub-zero spray pump.** Liquid from the frozen cartridge is sprayed over the whole
  bottle, not just the part touching a cold plate.
- **Frozen cartridge, not a compressor.** No refrigerant loop, no heat dumped into your
  kitchen, no compressor hum, nothing to service.
- **Infrared temperature sensor.** Reads the bottle and ends the cycle on its own. You do
  not set a timer; you set a temperature.
- **One aluminium dial, 2°C to 8°C.** Ice-blue LED ring around it so you can read the
  setting from across the room.
- **Clear dome lid.** You can see it working. That is half the reason people keep it out
  on the counter.
- **Internal battery, USB-C charging.** Works on a desk with a laptop cable. Works in the
  US and Europe with no adapter.
- **Brushed aluminium body, one piece.** About 28 × 14 × 14 cm. Wipes clean.

## 16. Bundle pricing — the argument, and what it needs

**Flag, in bold, for Alex: none of this is final. Pricing is decided from landed cost and
the landed cost is not known.** The tiers below are the shape I am arguing for and the
condition each one needs to be true.

**The argument.** The Bible says every household in America — a middle-class home, a
person who is broke, a rapper, Kim Kardashian. That sentence prices the product, and
$149–199 fails it. $149 is a considered purchase: a person on a tight budget researches
it for a week and buys nothing. **$89 is an impulse.** It is under the hundred-dollar
line, it is Shark-Tank money, it is a gift somebody buys without asking permission from
the household budget, and it is the number that lets a Meta ad scale. The product already
*looks* like $249 — brushed aluminium, a glowing dial, a glass dome. The gap between what
it looks like and what it costs is the entire conversion mechanism. Widening that gap is
worth more than the extra $60 of margin.

**Proposed tiers and their logic:**

- **the one — $89.** The entry, and the ad price. One machine, one cartridge. Every
  headline and every hook quotes this number.
- **the everyday — $109, most popular, default selected.** $20 buys two extra cartridges
  that are worth $38 bought separately. That is the classic bundle step: a small dollar
  jump for an obviously bigger pile. This is the tier that carries the AOV.
- **the household — $189.** Two machines. Cheaper than two of *the one*, framed as
  kitchen + office, or one for each kid. **$189, not $178** — the second machine is a
  gift or a second room, and it is not price-shopped as hard as the first.
- **Spare cartridges — $19 each, $45 for three.** The repeat purchase and the reason the
  machine keeps earning. Kept deliberately cheap; a $25 cartridge makes people ration
  their use of the machine, which kills the habit that sells the second one.

**Discounts are in dollars, never percentages.** Launch code: **$15 OFF**, never "15%
off". Platform rule 6.

**What the landed cost has to be for this to work.** Landed cost is unit price + tooling
amortised + shipping + duty, per machine at the door.

| Landed cost per machine | What we can do |
|---|---|
| **≤ $30** | $89 works comfortably. Roughly 66% gross margin, which is the room a cold-traffic Meta store needs to pay for a customer and still bank money. This is the target. Push the factories here. |
| **$31–38** | $89 is tight but survivable if the cartridges carry margin and *the everyday* is the default tier. Watch CPA weekly. |
| **$39–48** | $89 stops working on paid traffic. Either the price goes to $109 (and the Bible gets a little worse) or the spec gets simplified until the cost comes down. |
| **≥ $49** | The product as specified does not serve the brief. Change the build, not the brief. |

**The one question to every factory, before any of this is decided:** unit price at 500 /
1,000 / 3,000 units, MOQ, tooling cost, sample cost and lead time — and the measured
chill time for a 500 ml PET bottle from room temperature to 4°C. The time decides the ads
and the cost decides the price, and we have neither yet.
