# Growth

Instructions for the machine. Listing cadence, refresh timing, offers to likers,
response-time targets and the ban-by-behaviour rule are in `SKILL.md` and are the
hard constraints on everything below. This file is about **demand**: followers, the
look of the shop, other platforms, drops, and the social feed that fills it.

The ordering principle: **anything that raises conversion rate beats anything that
raises impressions.** Depop ranks on conversion, and every impression tactic that
does not convert actively lowers the shop's standing.

Researched September 2026.

---

## 1. What actually moves the needle, ranked

Ranked by effect per hour of Alex's time. Do these in order; do not start #4 before
#1 is true.

1. **Enough live listings.** Nothing works with a thin shop. Minimum **10 well-shot
   listings before promoting anywhere**, target **60–80 live** in Book A. A full shop
   page converts visitors to followers at roughly **twice the rate** of a sparse one.
2. **Cover photography consistency.** The single most-cited reason buyers skip a
   profile is bad photos. Same background family, same light, same crop across the
   grid, so the shop reads as one shop from the profile view.
3. **Reply speed.** A ranking input and a conversion input at once. Target under
   4 hours always, under 2 hours in European daytime, minutes when possible. Reply to
   **every** comment and DM within 24 hours without exception.
4. **The 1,000-follower threshold.** Reported effects, all single-source and
   directional: shops over 1,000 followers move inventory **3–5× faster** than shops
   under 200; crossing 1,000 correlates with **same-day sale rates up to 60% higher**;
   1,000+ correlates with up to **3× daily item views** vs under 100. Treat 1,000 as
   the first real milestone and build toward it deliberately.
5. **One short-form video a week**, cross-posted. Reported as the highest-ROI external
   channel in 2026.
6. **Targeted following inside the niche** — see §2 for the numbers and the hard
   safety limit.
7. **Cross-listing** to a second and third platform — see §4.
8. **Drops** — see §5. Only once the shop has an audience to drop to.

**Follower quality beats count.** An engaged 1,000 outperforms a passive 5,000.
Shops with no defined identity retain about **20%** of the followers gained in an
algorithmic spike; shops with a clear identity retain about **60%**.

## 2. Following, and the line not to cross

Targeted following works. Follow people who **like items similar to his**, and the
**followers of shops selling the same thing** — these are proven Depop buyers in the
niche, not random accounts.

**Rate: 20–30 follows per day, spread across the day, at human pace.**

**Hard safety limit, from `SKILL.md`:** enforcement is by behaviour. A reported case
hit a rate-limit ban in six hours at roughly **40 follow actions per minute**. The
app must never exceed a human cadence — no bursts, no even intervals, no overnight
runs, and never more than the daily figure above.

**Follow-for-follow is mostly cargo cult.** It produces short-term numbers and
low-quality followers; reciprocal followers are only about **20–30% likelier** to buy.
Do it as a side effect of targeted following, never as the strategy.

**Never buy followers or use a follower bot.** It is an account-ending behaviour
pattern, the followers never buy, and it poisons the conversion rate that the ranking
actually reads.

## 3. Shop aesthetic

The profile page is the shop window and it is judged in two seconds.

- **Bio**: one line on what the shop is, one on shipping origin, one on bundles.
  Complete, niche-specific bios convert at roughly **twice** the rate of empty ones.
  Include the niche words a buyer would search — "y2k, 90s vintage, workwear, designer
  — ships worldwide from greece · 10% off 2+".
- **Grid coherence**: consistent backgrounds, similar lighting, uniform angles and
  crops. One look. If a cover photo breaks the grid, reshoot it rather than list it.
- **Two books, one shop, no conflict.** Do not build a second account. Interleave: the
  designer pieces read as the shop's best pieces, not as a different business. The
  photography standard is what makes them belong together.
- **Never**: mirror selfies as covers, cluttered backgrounds, a hanger against a messy
  room, flash at night. Budget-looking photography correlates with budget pricing;
  professional-looking setups correlate with **2–3× higher** asking prices for the
  same category.
- **Profile picture**: the same across Depop, Instagram and TikTok so the accounts read
  as one brand.

## 4. Cross-listing

Rule of thumb that governs the whole decision: **a 10% fee on a sale in 3 days beats a
5% fee on a sale in 3 months.** Do not optimise for the lowest fee.

Average serious reseller uses **2.8 platforms**. Target three.

| Platform | Seller cost (2026) | Use it for | Notes for a Greek seller |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depop** | **10% + PayPal (~3%)** for Greece | Y2K, vintage, Gen Z womenswear, the whole Book A | Home base. Highest velocity for this product on this audience. |
| **Vinted** | **0% to the seller**; buyer pays ~5% + €0.70 Buyer Protection | Plain vintage, volume, EU buyers | Reported as the top earner for **65%** of resellers. Strong in Europe, which is Alex's shipping advantage. Lower prices for the same item — so list Vinted at a lower price and accept it. |
| **Grailed** | **6%** under $120, **9%** at $120+ (min $1.99), plus Stripe 3.49%+$0.49 domestic / **4.99%+$0.49 international** | Chrome Hearts, Carhartt, Nike, archive, all of Book B menswear | ~9.5% all-in under $120, ~12.5% at $120+ for international payouts. The right audience for high-ticket streetwear, and buyers there expect measurements and detail shots. |
| **eBay** | Clothing final value fee **~13.6% + $0.30/$0.40 per order**; some clothing categories quoted at **15.3%** (sources disagree — verify per category in his account before pricing) | Anything with a brand name and deep comp history; sneakers; the long tail | Also the best **sold-comp database** even when not selling there. |

**Where to put what:**
- Book A (Y2K/vintage/thrift) → Depop first, Vinted second. Skip Grailed.
- Book B streetwear (Carhartt, Nike, Dickies, Chrome Hearts apparel) → Depop **and**
  Grailed simultaneously.
- Book B designer bags → Depop plus a dedicated luxury platform, not Grailed.
- Everything over €150 → also eBay, for the comp depth and the buyer pool.

**The double-sale risk is the whole danger of cross-listing.** If the same physical
item sells twice, one buyer gets cancelled, and cancellations damage standing on every
platform. Rules:
1. One **SKU per physical item**, present in every platform's listing (in the
   description footer is fine).
2. When an item sells anywhere, **delist everywhere within 30 minutes.** This is a
   task the app should surface at the top of the queue, above everything else.
3. Do not cross-list **one-of-one high-value pieces** to more than two platforms. The
   cancellation risk outweighs the reach.

## 5. Drops

A drop is a scheduled batch release. It works because it concentrates demand into a
window instead of spreading it across a week, and it gives the social feed something
to announce.

**Format that fits this shop:**
- **One drop a week**, same day, same time. Pick a slot inside the peak windows in
  `SKILL.md` and never move it.
- **8–15 pieces** per drop for Book A. One or two Book B pieces per drop maximum — a
  designer piece should be announced on its own, not buried in a rail.
- **Announce 24 hours ahead** on Instagram Stories and TikTok, with a preview of 3–4
  pieces, then again 1 hour before.
- The drop does **not** replace baseline cadence. `SKILL.md` requires 3–5 new listings
  a week minimum; a weekly drop of 8–15 satisfies that and then some, but the app must
  still post something between drops so the shop never looks dormant.
- **Never** hold back stock for more than 7 days to make a drop bigger. Inventory
  sitting off-platform earns nothing and the dead-money clock in
  `01-sourcing-and-margin.md` is already running.

**Do not do "everything must go" energy.** The content that converts in 2026 reads as
*"what I'm adding this month"*, not as a clearance.

## 6. Instagram and TikTok feeding the shop

**TikTok is the highest-ROI external channel in 2026.** Reported figures, all
single-source and directional:
- Shop-tour content: **150–400 new followers per month** for a new Depop seller.
- A video catching an algorithmic boost: **200–400 new followers in 48 hours**.
- Consistent cross-platform promotion plus optimised listing cadence grows Depop
  followers **3–5× faster** than organic discovery alone.
- Cross-post to at least **two** external platforms; TikTok and Instagram Reels both
  drive measurable referral traffic.
- Days 1–30 of serious effort: expect **50–150** new followers. Days 61–90 with video:
  **15–40%** follower spikes.

**Content formats, in order of what works for this shop:**
1. **Rail tour / drop preview** — pieces on a rail, hands moving through them, one
   line each. This is the format that converts because it is the shop.
2. **One piece, styled, on a body, 8–12 seconds.** Fashion-designer eye is Alex's
   unfair advantage here; almost no reseller can style.
3. **"How I'd wear this three ways."** Highest saves per view of any format for
   womenswear vintage.
4. **Detail porn on Book B** — macro on a Carhartt union tag, a Chrome Hearts stamp,
   a Levi's big E. Slow, quiet, no voiceover. This is what sells the €600 jacket and
   it doubles as the authenticity evidence from
   `04-authenticity-and-risk.md`.
5. **Sourcing / "a plug's week"** — the thing nobody else can film. Use sparingly and
   never show a supplier's face, location or price.

**Rules:**
- Post the **link in bio to the Depop shop**, and name the shop in the caption; do not
  rely on viewers searching.
- Do not post the same video to both platforms at the same minute. Stagger by hours.
- Never use another person's audio-plus-video edit. Original footage only — the same
  rule as listing photos.
- One video a week is the floor. Three a week is where the compounding starts.

## 7. What is cargo-culted — stop doing or never start

- **Boosted Listings at 12%.** One 18-month seller account reports **+12% impressions
  and flat ROI**. For a Greek seller it stacks on top of the existing 10% + PayPal,
  pushing an order past **25%** in fees. **Default: off.** Only consider boosting a
  Book B piece already proven to convert, and only when the margin absorbs 25%+.
- **Refreshing the whole shop.** Explicitly penalised per `SKILL.md`. Refresh the
  15–20 most-liked items only.
- **Follower bots and bought followers.** Account-ending behaviour, zero buyers,
  wrecks conversion rate.
- **Follow/unfollow churn.** Low-quality followers, and the action volume is exactly
  the ban pattern.
- **Blast-messaging likers.** Offers to likers on the `SKILL.md` cadence, yes.
  Unsolicited DMs to people who have not engaged, never.
- **Generic hashtags** (#vintage, #fashion, #ootd). Millions of listings, reads as
  spam, ranks nothing. Specific only.
- **Emoji-heavy "🔥 RARE 🔥" captions.** Wrong shop voice and it suppresses the
  higher-ticket half of the business.
- **A second account for designer.** Splits the follower base, halves the cadence
  signal on both, doubles the work. One shop.
- **Chasing viral.** Search now drives between **48% and 65%** of TikTok Shop sales
  (early-2026 seller data, TikTok Shop specifically — directionally relevant, not
  Depop). Consistent searchable content beats one hit.
- **Discounting to buy velocity early.** Cheap pricing on a new shop trains the
  audience and attracts the lowest-quality buyers, who are also the likeliest to open
  disputes. Price correctly and wait.

## 8. The 90-day plan

Concrete, so the app can measure against it.

**Days 1–30 — build the shelf.**
- Get to **40 live listings**, then keep 3–5/week minimum.
- Full bio, consistent grid, all 5 hashtags on every listing.
- Follow 20–30 targeted accounts/day.
- Reply to everything inside 4 hours.
- Turn on the 10%-off-2+ bundle.
- One TikTok/Reel per week.
- Expect: **50–150 followers**, first sales in week 1–2 on Book A.

**Days 31–60 — engagement and the second platform.**
- 60–80 live listings.
- Add **Vinted** for Book A and **Grailed** for Book B streetwear. SKU everything.
- Start the weekly drop, same day, same hour.
- Offers to likers on the `SKILL.md` cadence.
- Dead-money ladder from `01-sourcing-and-margin.md` starts biting on the first cohort.
- Expect: approach **500 followers**; Book A STR should be readable by now.

**Days 61–90 — amplify.**
- Two to three short-form videos a week.
- First Book B piece announced on its own with detail-macro content.
- Add eBay for anything over €150 and use it for comps regardless.
- Expect: **1,000 followers** is the target to cross by day 90; **15–40%** follower
  spikes from video weeks.

**Metrics the app reports weekly:** live listings, new listings this week, sell-through
by book, median days to sale, follower count and delta, message response median,
offers sent / accepted, dead-stock count at 30/60/90 days, fee load as a % of gross.

## 9. Disagreements between sources, and what to trust

- **Relist frequency.** One source says relist unsold items every 2–3 weeks for up to
  **40% more profile visits/month**; another says wait **30 days**; the inventory
  guidance says delist/relist at **60 days**. **Trust 60 days** for a full
  delist/relist, and keep the SKILL.md daily-refresh rule separate from it. Relisting
  destroys accumulated likes, which are the free offer audience.
- **Follower-count effects (3–5× faster, 60% higher same-day sales, 3× views).** All
  from seller-blog content with no published methodology. Directionally believable,
  numerically **unverified**. Do not quote them to Alex as facts.
- **eBay clothing fee: 13.6% vs 15.3%.** Sources disagree. **Verify in his own account
  per category** before pricing anything for eBay.
- **Bundle AOV lift of 30–50%.** Vendor-published, **unverified**. The direction is
  sound; the magnitude is marketing.
- **Depop fee 0% vs 10%.** Resolved in `01-sourcing-and-margin.md`: for Greece it is
  **10%**, and almost every English-language Depop growth blog is written for a US/UK
  seller who pays 0%. Recheck every fee claim against his own payout screen.
