# Listing Craft

Instructions for the machine that writes the listing. The title formula, hashtag
count, photo order and refresh cadence live in `SKILL.md` and are not repeated here.
This file is about the **words and the numbers inside the listing**: how to write for
search and for a human in the same breath, what to measure, how to grade condition,
and how to describe a flaw so it cannot become a claim.

Researched September 2026.

---

## 1. Two readers, two jobs

Every listing is read twice.

**The search index reads the title first, then hashtags and category, then the
description.** It wants nouns: brand, garment, era, colour, cut, size.

**The human reads the cover photo, then the first line of the description, then the
measurements.** They want to know if it will fit them and whether it is worn out.

So: **the title is written for the index, the first line of the description is written
for the human, and the rest of the description is written for the index again.**

Never write a title that reads like a sentence. Never write a first description line
that reads like a keyword list.

### The keyword sweep

Before writing, list every word a buyer might type for this piece, then make sure each
appears **somewhere** in the listing — title if it is one of the top four, description
if not. For a single garment that is typically 12–20 terms:

- Brand, and its common misspellings/variants (Levi's / Levis, Carhartt / Carhart)
- Sub-line (WIP, 501, Detroit, Blank Canvas)
- Garment noun and its synonyms (jumper / sweater, trousers / pants, trainers /
  sneakers) — **use both the UK and US word** because his buyers are both
- Era (90s, Y2K, 2000s, vintage, 80s)
- Cut/fit (baggy, low rise, bootcut, cropped, oversized, boxy, slim)
- Colour, including the fashion word (butter yellow, not yellow; chocolate, not brown)
- Material (leather, suede, denim, cotton, mesh, lace, knit)
- Aesthetic tag (grunge, coquette, whimsygoth, blokecore, gorpcore, streetwear)
- Size, both label and equivalent (M, UK 10, EU 38, W32)

**Do not keyword-stuff.** Repeating a word five times or listing unrelated brands
("not Nike Adidas Supreme") is penalised in ranking and reads as a scam shop.
One clean pass of each term.

## 2. Description templates

Three templates. Pick by book. All are written to be spoken aloud in Alex's voice —
warm, short sentences, lowercase-friendly, no marketplace-speak, no "beautiful piece!!
🥰 grab it fast!!".

Depop's description field is generous; use **120–220 words**. Under 80 words looks
lazy; over 300 nobody reads.

### Template A — Vintage / Y2K / thrift (Book A)

```
[One line of feeling. What it is and who it's for. No brand-speak.]

[Brand] · [era] · [material]
Labelled size [X] — fits like a [range]

Pit to pit: XX in / XX cm
Length: XX in / XX cm
[garment-specific third measurement]

Condition: [grade]. [Every flaw, named and located.]

[One styling line — what it goes with.]

Ships from Greece, tracked. Bundle 2+ for 10% off.
#tag #tag #tag #tag #tag
```

Worked example:

> the exact butter-yellow baby tee everyone's looking for and nobody can find.
>
> no brand tag · early 2000s · cotton/elastane
> labelled S — fits XS–S, very cropped
>
> pit to pit: 15 in / 38 cm
> length: 15.5 in / 39 cm
>
> condition: very good. tiny pull on the left sleeve seam, photo 4. no stains, no
> smell, colour is still strong.
>
> wears well with low rise denim and something big over the top.
>
> ships from greece, tracked. bundle 2+ for 10% off.

### Template B — Streetwear (Book B, lower half)

Lead with the identifying facts, because this buyer is checking you know what you have.

```
[Brand] [model/sub-line] [garment], [year or era if datable, and how you know].

Tag: [exact tag text / colourway code / date code]
Made in: [country]
Size: [label]. Modern equivalent: [size].

Pit to pit XX in / XX cm
Length XX in / XX cm
Shoulder XX in / XX cm
Sleeve XX in / XX cm

Condition: [grade]. [Flaws.] [Honest wear note — fading, hardware, hem.]

[One line on why it's good — the detail a buyer who knows the brand cares about.]

Ships from Greece, tracked with signature. Bundle 2+ for 10% off.
#tag #tag #tag #tag #tag
```

Worked example:

> Carhartt Detroit jacket, blanket lined. Union label + Made in USA, so pre-1994.
>
> Tag: J97 BRN, white vinyl hem tag
> Made in: USA
> Size: labelled 42R. Wears like a modern L/XL, boxy.
>
> pit to pit 25 in / 63.5 cm
> length 27 in / 68.5 cm
> shoulder 21 in / 53 cm
> sleeve 25 in / 63.5 cm
>
> Condition: good, honest workwear wear. Duck has faded to that pale tan around the
> elbows and hem (photo 3). Small paint fleck on the right cuff. Corduroy collar
> intact, no fraying. Zip runs clean end to end, tested. Blanket lining complete, no
> holes.
>
> The pre-94 union tag and the original zip are the two things people check on these.
> Both here.
>
> Ships from Greece, tracked with signature. Bundle 2+ for 10% off.

### Template C — Designer (Book B, top half)

This one is a provenance document that happens to also be a listing. It is defensive
writing: everything in it exists so a buyer cannot later say they were surprised.

```
[Brand] [model] in [material/colourway]. [Year, if hardware or stamp establishes it.]

Provenance: [where it came from, what came with it]
Included: [box / dust bag / clochette / receipt / cards / spare parts]
Hallmarks photographed: [list exactly which stamps and where, matched to photo #]

Measurements: [L x W x H, drop, strap]

Condition: [grade]. [Every mark, corner, scuff, stretch, odour, hardware tarnish,
interior, smell, and what it does NOT have.]

Sold as photographed and measured. Happy to send more photos of any detail before you
buy — ask and I'll shoot it the same day.

Ships from Greece, fully insured, tracked, signature required.
#tag #tag #tag #tag #tag
```

**Never write, in any template:** "100% authentic", "guaranteed genuine", "authenticity
guaranteed", "legit checked" as a bare claim. Those are express warranties and they
survive any "as-is" line you write later; they convert a disagreement into a
liability. Write instead: *"Photographed in detail so you can check every hallmark
yourself — stamps in photos 5–8."* Show, do not swear. See
`04-authenticity-and-risk.md`.

## 3. Measurements — exactly what to take, per garment

**Rules that apply to everything:**
- Lay the garment **flat**, smoothed, seams squared, zips closed, before measuring.
- Measure **flat / across**, and say so. Do not double silently. Either write
  "pit to pit 20 in (flat)" or write "chest 40 in (all round)" — never a bare number.
- **Both units, every time: inches then cm.** His buyers are US (inches), UK (both)
  and EU (cm). `20 in / 51 cm`.
- Round to the nearest **0.5 in / 1 cm**. Do not write 19.87.
- Write "approx." once at the top of the block, not on every line.
- **Photograph one flat lay with the tape measure visible.** It kills half the
  messages and it is the single best evidence in a dispute.
- Never copy a size chart. Measure the garment in hand. A vintage M is not a modern M.

### Tee / top / baby tee
1. Pit to pit
2. Length (high point of shoulder, straight down to hem)
3. Shoulder to shoulder (seam to seam across the back)
4. Sleeve (shoulder seam to cuff end)
For cropped/fitted: add **waist (flat)**. For stretch fabric: give **unstretched and
max stretched** waist.

### Jacket / coat / outerwear
1. Pit to pit
2. Length (back of collar to hem — say "back length" for outerwear, it is the
   convention)
3. Shoulder to shoulder
4. Sleeve (shoulder seam to cuff)
5. Waist (flat, at narrowest)
Also state: **lining** (present/absent/type), **closure** (zip, buttons, both) and
whether it has been tested.

### Denim / trousers
Use **WHIRL**: Waist, Hips, Inseam, Rise, Length.
1. Waist (flat across top of waistband — state flat, or double and say "all round")
2. Hips (flat, at widest, usually 7–8 in below waistband)
3. Rise (front crotch seam up to top of waistband)
4. Inseam (crotch seam down inner leg to hem)
5. Outseam / total length (top of waistband to hem)
6. Leg opening (flat, across the hem) — this is what tells a buyer if it's bootcut,
   straight or baggy, and almost nobody includes it. Include it.
For elastic waistbands: **unstretched and stretched**.
Always state the **label size** separately from the tape (`labelled W30 — measures
32 in flat waist`); vintage denim lies constantly.

### Dress / skirt
1. Pit to pit (dress) or waist (skirt)
2. Waist (flat, narrowest)
3. Hips (flat, widest)
4. Length — dress: shoulder to hem; skirt: waistband to hem
5. Strap drop, and whether adjustable
For a flowing hem, measure the hem flat and **double it**, and say you doubled it.

### Knitwear
As tee, plus a note: **give the measurement unstretched** and say the knit has give.
A stretched measurement on knitwear is the most common honest cause of a "not as
described".

### Bag
Format is **Length × Width (depth) × Height**, in that order — the universally read
order. Then:
- **Strap drop** (shoulder to top of bag) and total strap length
- Whether the strap is adjustable / removable
- Hardware colour and plating
- Interior: pockets, lining condition, smell
- Whether it stands unaided

### Shoes / sneakers
1. **Labelled size, and the region** (UK 9 / US 10 / EU 43) — read all three off the
   tongue label and photograph it
2. **Insole length, heel to toe, in cm** — this is the real size and the only number
   that survives brand-to-brand variation
3. Heel height (heels/boots)
4. Boots also: shaft height, shaft circumference, boot opening
5. Sole condition and tread depth, in words, with a photo of both soles
6. Box: present or not

## 4. Condition grading

Use exactly five grades. Use the same five every time so the shop reads as one voice
and a buyer learns what the words mean.

| Grade | Means | Write it as |
|---|---|---|
| **Deadstock / NWT** | Never worn, original tags attached, no storage flaws | "deadstock, tags on" |
| **Excellent** | Worn a handful of times. No flaw a buyer would find on inspection. | "excellent — barely worn, nothing to flag" |
| **Very good** | Gently worn. Minor, inconspicuous flaws, all disclosed. | "very good — [flaw], otherwise clean" |
| **Good** | Visible wear consistent with age. Real flaws, all disclosed and photographed. | "good, honest vintage wear — [flaws]" |
| **Fair** | Apparent wear, stains, repairs, or damage. Priced accordingly. Only list Fair when the piece is rare enough to be worth it. | "fair — [damage]. Priced for it." |

Rules:
- **Grade one step down from your instinct.** Overgrading produces returns and reviews;
  undergrading produces a delighted buyer and a five-star review. The cost of the two
  errors is not symmetrical.
- Never write "like new", "mint", "perfect", "flawless", "9/10", "immaculate". They are
  unfalsifiable, they promise, and they invite the claim.
- Never write "no flaws" unless you have inspected in daylight, inside out, under the
  arms, along every seam, and at every hem.
- **Vintage wear is not a flaw and should not be apologised for** — it is the product.
  Distinguish *patina* (fading, softening, honest wear) from *damage* (holes, stains,
  broken hardware). Sell the first; disclose the second.

## 5. Flaws — how to say it

The pattern: **name it, locate it, size it, show it, and say whether it affects wear.**

```
[what] on the [where], about [size]. [Photo N]. [Effect on wear.]
```

Examples:
- "small hole at the left underarm seam, about 5 mm. photo 5. doesn't run, easy repair
  if you want it gone."
- "faint mark under the right arm, about 2 cm, didn't come out in a wash. photo 6.
  invisible when worn."
- "the collar has softened and rolls a bit — this is normal on 90s cotton and it's not
  damage."
- "one belt loop has been restitched by a previous owner. photo 7. holds fine."
- "zip pull replaced with a generic one, original is gone. photo 4."

Say what you **checked and did not find**, because the absence is worth money:
"no smell, no underarm staining, no repairs, colour is unfaded."

A flaw that is disclosed in words **and** photographed is nearly impossible to build a
"significantly not as described" case on. A flaw that is photographed but not written
is weak evidence. A flaw that is written but not photographed is weaker. Do both,
always, and reference the photo number in the sentence so a reviewer can follow it.

**Smell, pets and smoke must be disclosed.** These are the most common undisclosed
"not as described" grounds and they are indefensible. "Smoke-free, pet-free home" if
true; "stored in a home with a cat, washed before shipping" if not.

**Alterations, resizing, taken-in seams, shortened hems and reworked pieces must be
disclosed as alterations** even when they look factory. An undisclosed alteration on a
designer piece is treated as a misrepresentation.

## 6. Sizing the human, not the label

State three things, always, in this order:

1. **Labelled size** — exactly as printed, including if there is no tag ("no size tag").
2. **What it measures** — the tape.
3. **What it fits** — a range, with a reason.

> labelled M (90s cut) — measures 21 in pit to pit — fits a modern L/XL boxy, or an M
> oversized

Never say "fits everyone", "one size", or "true to size" on vintage. Never guess a
modern equivalent from the label alone; derive it from the tape.

If the item is modelled, say the model's height and usual size:
"worn by 5'7 / 170 cm, usually a UK 8."

## 7. Listing QA — run before publishing

Refuse to publish if any of these fail.

1. Title follows the SKILL.md formula and contains brand + garment + era + one more
   specific term.
2. All five hashtags used, all specific, none generic.
3. Category and sub-category set correctly (it is a ranking input, not decoration).
4. Photo order matches SKILL.md; flaw photo present if any flaw exists.
5. One flat-lay photo with a tape measure visible.
6. Every required measurement for that garment type is present, in **both units**.
7. Condition grade is one of the five words, and a flaw list exists or the words
   "checked for X, Y, Z — none found" appear.
8. Description contains no banned phrase: *100% authentic, guaranteed, mint, perfect,
   flawless, like new, no returns, all sales final, price firm, rare!!, must go.*
9. Shipping origin stated ("ships from Greece") and tracking stated.
10. Price is **median sold comp + 10–15%** (see `01-sourcing-and-margin.md`), and a
    floor has been recorded internally for negotiation (see
    `03-messages-and-negotiation.md`).
11. For any designer piece: hallmark/stamp photos exist and are referenced by number
    in the description.

## 8. What NOT to put in a listing

- **"No returns" / "all sales final".** Unenforceable — the platform's policy overrides
  a shop bio or a listing line, and writing it makes a buyer likelier to open a dispute
  rather than message you. Say nothing about returns in the listing.
- **Price-firm language.** "Price firm, no offers" kills the offer flow, and offers are
  how Depop items actually close. Let them offer; handle it in messages.
- **Emoji walls, all-caps, "🔥🔥 RARE 🔥🔥".** Reads as a flip shop, which is the one
  thing the shop is not supposed to read as.
- **Other brands' names as bait** ("similar to Chrome Hearts", "Carhartt vibes",
  "Birkin style"). This is trademark misuse, it is a takedown trigger, and it is
  exactly the pattern Depop's IP enforcement looks for.
- **Stock photos or other sellers' photos.** Every photo must be his. Reused photos
  are both an IP report and the strongest possible evidence against him in a dispute.
- **"DM for price" / "ask me".** Depop ranks on conversion; a listing you cannot buy
  converts at zero.
- **Contact details, external links, off-platform payment.** Off-platform payment
  removes every protection and is grounds for suspension on both sides.
