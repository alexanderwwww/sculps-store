# Q4: the calendar every recommendation is judged against

Q4 is where the year's money is made, and it is also when a late product
loses everything. A product is only a recommendation if it can be bought,
shipped, advertised and delivered before its moment. Do the date arithmetic
from today's date every time; never assume it.

## The 2026 dates (US)
- **Halloween:** Saturday 31 October.
- **Singles' Day (China):** 11 November. Suppliers and Chinese logistics are
  slower around it.
- **Thanksgiving:** Thursday 26 November. **Black Friday:** 27 November.
  **Cyber Monday:** 30 November. BFCM runs roughly 20 November to 1 December.
- **Christmas:** Friday 25 December. USPS's recommended send-by dates for the
  lower 48 (from usps.com/holiday, announced September 2026): Ground Advantage
  and First-Class 17 December, Priority Mail 18 December, Priority Mail
  Express 19 December. These are recommendations, not guarantees.
- **Chinese New Year 2027:** 6 February. Factories slow down from late January
  and output is not normal again until March. Anything for Q1 2027 is ordered
  and shipped in December or early January. (Sourcing guides, not official
  notices; confirm with each supplier.)

## How Q4 changes the judgement
1. **Count the days back from the moment.** Days left = moment minus today.
   Subtract supplier processing time and shipping time from the supplier's
   own page, plus about a week to launch ads and the store. If what remains is
   less than two weeks of selling, it is not a Q4 product. It is a
   next-year product. Say so.
2. **Where the stock ships from decides everything in December.** From China
   on standard lines, the Christmas cut-off for orders arrives in early
   December. From a US warehouse it is the USPS dates above. The report says
   which one a supplier is and what that means for the last order day.
3. **Gifts win.** From November the buyer is often not the user. Products that
   are easy to give, look good in a box, sit under about $50, and need no
   sizing beat products that need explaining. Bundles (1+1, a gift set) do well.
4. **Ads cost more from mid-November to Christmas.** Every advertiser is
   bidding, so the same budget buys fewer views. The margin check in
   `margin-math.md` must survive a higher cost per sale in BFCM and December.
   A product that only just works in October fails in November.
5. **Seasonal products die on a date.** Halloween stops on 1 November and
   Christmas decor on 26 December. A seasonal product's revenue estimate stops
   on that date. Evergreen gifts keep selling into January.
6. **After Christmas comes a new list:** New Year (fitness, organising, self
   improvement), then Valentine's Day (14 February). In late December the
   hunt moves to these, with the Chinese New Year shutdown in the sums.

## Where it shows in the report
Every recommended product carries: its moment (or "evergreen"), days left,
the last day to order stock, where it ships from, and whether it still makes
money at Q4 ad costs. If any of those cannot be worked out from what was seen,
the report says so.
