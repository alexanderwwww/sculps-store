# Reading the Ad Library

The Meta Ad Library is the only place where somebody else's money tells you
the truth. Everything else — views, likes, comments — is free to fake and free
to get. An ad that has been running for six weeks is an ad that is paying for
itself, because nobody keeps paying to lose money.

## What to actually look at

- **Started running on** — the only date that matters. Convert it to days.
- **How many advertisers** are running the same product, not the same ad. Five
  different sellers past a month is a market. One seller past a year is a moat
  you are not getting into.
- **How many active ads one advertiser has** for the product. A seller running
  40 variations is scaling; a seller running one is testing.
- **The copy and the angle**, not the creative. The angle is what you can
  copy legally; the creative you must make yourself.

## The thresholds

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Nothing past 14 days | Unproven. Not a finding, a guess. |
| One or two past 28 days | Real but thin. Watch it, don't bet the month. |
| Three or more past 28 days | A working market. This is what you want. |
| Everyone past 180 days | Mature. You are late unless your angle is new. |

## What to refuse

- Reporting an advertiser name that was not on the page.
- Treating a start date you could not read as "new". Unknown is unknown.
- Counting the same ad twice because it appeared in two scrolls.
